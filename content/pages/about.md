# About

I'm Diego — a **cloud architect** who still writes the code, building event-driven systems on AWS and spending the quiet hours chasing vintage guitar tones.

What I get paid to decide is architectural: where state lives, what happens when a boundary fails, what a system costs on a quiet Sunday, and whether someone else can operate it at 3 a.m. [The architecture page](#/page/cloud-architecture) makes that case with receipts.

Most of my work lives where reliability is earned, not assumed: message queues with dead-letter destinations, transactional outboxes, projections you can rebuild from scratch, and infrastructure that exists only as code. I run the Eleva platform — a school management system that powers itself down outside school hours, because the cheapest instance is the one that's off.

## What I believe about software

- **Boring on purpose.** Predictable systems beat clever ones at 3 a.m.
- **The log is the truth.** State is a cache; events are the record. This very site runs on that idea — every post and page you read here is a fold over an append-only log.
- **If it isn't in the repo, it doesn't exist.** Environments are programs. Mine have a power button.

## Where the foundation comes from

**B.Eng. in Computer Science — Universidad Técnica Federico Santa María (UTFSM), Chile.**

UTFSM is an engineering school in the old sense: theory first, and no sympathy for hand-waving. Data structures, algorithms, operating systems, networks, databases and the mathematics under all of it — taught as things you must be able to derive, not merely use.

That foundation is why I reach for the primitive rather than the framework when a system gets strange. Queues, logs, transactions, consistency models and failure modes aren't buzzwords I picked up from a conference talk; they're the vocabulary I was taught to think in. Frameworks age out every few years. Fundamentals are why the next one takes a weekend instead of a quarter.
